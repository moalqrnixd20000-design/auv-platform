Project overview

AUV stands for Autonomous Underwater Vehicle. The project is a vehicle concept built around the team's own design, intended to survey shallow water and support the inspection of objects found there.

The concept combines two ways of sensing the water, acoustic and visual, with software that is meant to flag candidate objects, analyse them and suggest what to inspect next. A person stays responsible for every decision.

The problem

Shallow coastal waters hold natural features, marine life and man-made objects side by side. Wrecks, debris and equipment matter for safety, for protecting the marine environment and for inspection work, but they are not always easy to tell apart from their surroundings.

Divers can inspect an area directly, but their time and reach are limited. Cameras depend on light and water clarity. Acoustic sensors keep working in poor visibility but give less visual detail. No single sensor answers the question “what is this object?” on its own.

The project asks how a small vehicle could combine both kinds of sensing, and support a human inspector by flagging objects of interest and explaining why they were flagged.

Proposed AUV solution

The proposal is a shallow-water AUV that scans an area, collects camera and acoustic data, and passes it to software that flags and analyses candidate objects. The software is meant to support decisions, not replace them: its results are described as potential objects or observations that need inspection.

Operating environment

The project's current operating scope is shallow water, 1–5 metres below the surface. This is the range the concept is being developed for. It is a project scope, not a depth rating: pressure and depth-rating specifications are still to be decided.

Depth scope
1–5 m below the water surface. The concept does not address deeper water. Confirmed
Marine context

Sensor and data layer

Three sensing elements are part of the concept. Each is described by its intended role only; the team has not yet chosen any hardware.

Underwater camera

Conceptual

Intended for visual inspection, for visually confirming detected targets, and for recognising a limited set of selected marine species.

Multibeam echosounder

Conceptual

Intended for seafloor mapping, bathymetric information and acoustic detection of potential targets.

Water column acoustic data

Conceptual

Intended for analysing acoustic returns within the water column, to help detect potential targets and support inspection. It does not by itself identify fish or objects.

How the sensors are meant to work together

Acoustic data can suggest that something is present; the camera can help show what it looks like. A given target may be seen by one sensor or by both.

Software and AI concept

The software layer is a plan, not a product. The components below describe what the team intends to build or demonstrate as a prototype. No trained model is connected to this website, and nothing on this page is a detection result.

Object detection
Flag candidate objects in camera and acoustic data. Planned / Prototype
Object analysis and classification
Describe a candidate and suggest a careful category such as “potential man-made object”, “potential anomaly” or “object of interest”. Planned / Prototype
Decision support
Show a suggested next step with the reasons behind it. A person makes the decision. Planned / Prototype
Species recognition
Recognise a limited list of selected Red Sea fish species. It is not a general species classifier and does not check where an image was taken. Planned / Prototype
Target identification and marking
Give each flagged target an identifier and mark it for inspection. Planned / Prototype
Wording rule

Results will be described as potential objects or observations that require inspection. The software will not claim that an object is definitely man-made, dangerous or illegal, and it will not claim to identify everything it sees.
