About USV B01

B01 is an autonomous surface vehicle concept designed to survey maritime environments, detect underwater objects and near-surface hazards, assess potential risks, and provide explainable, risk-prioritized information to support maritime safety and inspection decisions. It is based on the team's own B01 hull design and works at the water surface: it surveys an area autonomously, explains each decision and tells the operator which targets to inspect first, without putting people in danger.

The problem

Traditional maritime surveys and mine-hunting operations rely on large, crewed vessels. They put personnel at risk and cost a great deal to run, and in shallow coastal waters they raise many false alarms: marine life, natural features, wrecks, debris and old ordnance lie side by side, and most of these objects are not hazards.

Different sensing methods provide different types of information about underwater targets. Acoustic sensing can remain useful in poor visibility, while target interpretation still requires analysis of multiple characteristics. No single sensing source answers the question “what is this object, and is it a hazard?” on its own.

What the system does

USV B01 surveys the area autonomously with a grid (cross-hatch) method: a complete set of parallel survey lines, then a second complete set at right angles over the same area. Hull-mounted multibeam and side-scan sonar and water-column profiling look for underwater targets, and a sound velocity profiler (SVP) measures the sound-speed profile used to correct the acoustic measurements; RTK GNSS and the IMU give each target its position, while the navigation radar, AIS and an EO/IR camera watch the surface. Each target is analysed, classified and given a risk level, a risk type and a decision with an explanation. When the evidence is not enough, the system reports the object as unknown and asks for verification instead of forcing a class.

Targets are marked on the survey map, and only encrypted, risk-prioritized alerts are sent to the operator over RF (UHF), 4G and satellite links (proposed).

How it works

From the water to the operator: USV B01 senses the area below it, detects targets, classifies them, assesses their potential risk, explains each decision and recommends an action.

  1. Underwater Environment

    The water column and seabed below B01. Proposed survey depth: approximately 0–60 m. Designed to support surveys in shallow-water and deeper coastal environments. The achievable detection depth, minimum detection range, and effective coverage depend on the selected sensor models, installation geometry, water conditions, and field validation.

  2. Sensor / Acoustic Data

    Multibeam and side-scan sonar and water-column profiling, with SVP sound-speed correction, positioned with RTK GNSS and an IMU.

  3. Target Detection

    Candidate objects are found in the sensor data and given a target ID and a detection time.

  4. Target Feature Analysis

    Shape, geometry, structure, size category, movement and difference from the background are examined.

  5. Object Classification

    Marine life, man-made structure or potential hazardous object — or unknown when the evidence is not enough.

  6. Risk Assessment

    Likely non-hazardous, potential hazard, high potential hazard or unknown; every level except likely non-hazardous requires verification.

  7. Risk Type Identification

    For example navigation / obstruction risk or potential explosive hazard.

  8. Explainable Decision

    A decision together with the evidence and the rule that led to it.

  9. Target Marking

    The target is marked at its position on the survey map.

  10. Recommended Action

    Continue the survey, inspect the target, or verify it.

  11. Local Data Storage

    Target records and raw sensor data are stored on board, on SSD.

  12. Data Transfer / Operator System

    Encrypted, risk-prioritized alert packets reach the operator over RF (UHF), 4G and satellite links (proposed).

Sensors, computing, communication and power Proposed

Underwater sensing

Multibeam Sonar (MBES)

Hull-mounted multibeam transducers: seabed mapping, bathymetry and acoustic target detection.

Side-Scan Sonar (SSS)

High-resolution images of the seafloor on both sides of the track, showing the shape and acoustic shadow of objects.

Water-Column Profiling

Acoustic returns between the surface and the seabed, used to detect potential targets in the water column.

Sound Velocity Profiler (SVP)

Measures the sound-speed profile of the water column, used to correct the acoustic measurements (sonar ranges and depths). It does not detect targets itself.

Navigation and surface awareness

RTK GNSS

Precise position of the vessel, used to record the location of each target.

IMU

Roll, pitch, heave and heading of the vessel, used to correct the sonar data for the vessel's motion.

EO/IR Thermal Gimbal Camera

Electro-optical and infrared camera on a stabilised gimbal, for surface objects and thermal anomalies around the vessel.

Navigation Radar + AIS

Marine navigation radar shows surface traffic and obstacles, AIS identifies the ships that broadcast it, and navigation lights keep B01 visible to other vessels.

Communications: RF (UHF), 4G and satellite links (proposed)

Radio links connect B01 with the operator and the ground control station: RF radio (UHF), 4G and satellite (SatCom). Only encrypted, risk-prioritized alert packets are sent; raw data stays on board.

Encryption: we will use the latest available encryption version.

Classification, risk and decision

Each target is analysed from its shape, geometry, structure, size category, movement where applicable, acoustic characteristics and difference from the surrounding background. The classification, the risk assessment and the recommended action are kept as three separate results. Risk uses four levels, a four-level threat classification: high potential hazard, potential hazard and unknown, each requiring verification, and likely non-hazardous.

Likely Non-Hazardous

Marine life, and objects that show no sign of an immediate hazard. Decision: continue survey / log observation.

Potential Hazard — Requires Verification

Wrecks, obstructions and other man-made structures. Decision: requires inspection. A potential hazardous object is a high potential hazard; it always requires verification and is never reported as confirmed.

Unknown — Requires Verification

Objects without enough evidence for a reliable class. The system says “unknown” instead of guessing. Decision: requires verification.

Potential risk is expressed as a priority for the operator to review; the system does not determine danger with certainty. The decision engine in this prototype is rule-based and explainable. Trained recognition models are future development and are not part of it.

Four decision layers Conceptual

Every target is judged by four complementary layers. In the demo they are explainable rules over simulated evidence; a multi-stream AI model that combines them is future development.

Temporal analysis

How a target's echo changes over time, for example sway around a mooring point. Movement helps separate marine life from fixed or moored objects.

Environmental adaptation

Each return is compared with the local seabed and water-column background, so the decision adapts to the area. A return that stands out from its background is stronger evidence that an object is present. In the demo this comparison is a simulated evidence item.

Multi-angle consensus

  1. Primary pass (Pass A)
  2. Target detected
  3. Orthogonal pass (Pass B), perpendicular heading
  4. Evidence comparison
  5. Decision update

The grid is surveyed twice: a complete horizontal lawn-mower pass, then a complete perpendicular pass over the same area. Every target is observed from two survey headings about 90° apart, and the two observations are compared before the decision is updated. A second heading adds evidence; it does not guarantee a correct classification.

Explainable AI

Every decision shows the evidence and the rule behind it (in the concept: shape, shadow, sway and background). When the evidence is not enough, the answer is “Unknown” instead of a forced class, which is intended to reduce false alarms.

Data transfer Conceptual

On board, a network switch links the sensors to the Edge AI computer (GPU), which processes the data and keeps the raw data on SSD. Radio links carry limited data, especially over satellite, so only encrypted, risk-prioritized alert packets are sent, over RF (UHF), 4G and satellite links (proposed).

B01 design

USV B01 is built around the team's own hull design: a faceted, ultra-low-profile hull that works at the water surface. A short mast carries the EO/IR camera, the radar and the antennas, the sonar is mounted on the hull below, and the cutaway in the render shows the internal equipment bay. Next stage and future work

Next stage: prototype development and simulation-based validation of the multi-pass detection workflow.

  • Develop explainable AI for evidence-based threat classification.
  • Automate seabed surveying, coverage and target detection.
  • Improve detection accuracy through multi-pass verification and false-alarm reduction.
  • Enhance secure data management with encrypted alerts and efficient transmission.
  • Integrate smart navigation with real-time hazard alerts and safe route planning.
