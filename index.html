USV B01 is an unmanned surface vehicle concept for detecting naval mines and other underwater hazards. Working at the water surface, it surveys an area in a cross-hatch grid, scans the water column and the seabed below it with multibeam sonar, and gives the operator an explained decision and a recommended action for every target.

About USV B01

An unmanned surface vehicle built around the team's own B01 hull design. Working at the water surface, it surveys an area, detects underwater objects with sonar, assesses whether they may be hazardous, explains each decision and tells the operator which targets to inspect first.

The problem

Coastal and offshore waters hold marine life, natural features and man-made objects side by side: shipwrecks, debris, lost equipment and, in some areas, old ordnance. Some of these are hazards to navigation or to people working in the water; most are not.

Different sensing methods provide different types of information about underwater targets. Acoustic sensing can remain useful in poor visibility, while target interpretation still requires analysis of multiple characteristics. No single sensing source answers the question “what is this object, and is it a hazard?” on its own.

What the system does

USV B01 covers the survey area with a grid (cross-hatch) method: a complete set of parallel survey lines, then a second complete set at right angles over the same area. Its multibeam sonar and water-column acoustic data detect underwater targets, and RTK GNSS with the IMU gives each target its position. Each target is analysed, classified and given a risk level, a risk type and a decision with an explanation. When the evidence is not enough, the system reports the object as unknown and asks for verification instead of forcing a class.

Targets are marked on the survey map and reported to the operator over the vessel's radio links.

Survey environment

B01 works at the water surface. Its sonar looks down through the water column to the seabed, and the concept targets a survey depth band of 1–30 metres below the surface. This is a conceptual range; the vessel and its sensors have not been tested or certified across it.

How it works

From the water to the operator: USV B01 senses the area below it, detects targets, classifies them, assesses their potential risk, explains each decision and recommends an action.

  1. Underwater Environment

    The water column and seabed below B01, 1–30 m deep (concept).

  2. Sensor / Acoustic Data

    Multibeam sonar and water-column acoustic data, positioned with RTK GNSS and an IMU.

  3. Target Detection

    Candidate objects are found in the sensor data and given a target ID and a detection time.

  4. Target Feature Analysis

    Shape, geometry, structure, size category, movement and difference from the background are examined.

  5. Object Classification

    Marine life, man-made structure or potential hazardous object — or unknown when the evidence is not enough.

  6. Risk Assessment

    Non-hazardous, potential hazard (or high potential hazard), or unknown and requiring verification.

  7. Risk Type Identification

    For example navigation / obstruction risk or potential explosive hazard.

  8. Explainable Decision

    A decision together with the evidence and the rule that led to it.

  9. Target Marking

    The target is marked at its position on the survey map.

  10. Recommended Action

    Continue the survey, inspect the target, or verify it.

  11. Local Data Storage

    Target records and raw sensor data are stored on board.

  12. Data Transfer / Operator System

    Priority target records reach the operator over encrypted radio links: UHF, 4G or satellite.

Sensors, communication and power Proposed

Multibeam Sonar

A multibeam echosounder looking down from the hull: seabed mapping, bathymetry and acoustic target detection.

Water-Column Acoustic Data

Acoustic returns between the surface and the seabed, used to detect potential targets in the water column.

RTK GNSS

Precise position of the vessel, so every target is recorded with an accurate location.

IMU

Roll, pitch, heave and heading of the vessel, used to correct the sonar data for the vessel's motion.

Cameras

Daylight and thermal cameras on the mast, for the surface picture around the vessel.

Radar + AIS

Radar shows surface traffic and obstacles; AIS identifies the ships that broadcast it. Both support safe navigation.

Communications: UHF / 4G / Satellite

Three radio links connect B01 with the operator and the ground control station: UHF, 4G and satellite. Communication is separate from the sensing layer: the sensors observe the environment, the links carry the results.

Classification, risk and decision

Each target is analysed from its shape, geometry, structure, size category, movement where applicable, acoustic characteristics and difference from the surrounding background. The classification, the risk assessment and the recommended action are kept as three separate results.

Non-Hazardous

Marine life, and objects with no immediate hazard. Decision: continue survey / log observation.

Potential Hazard

Wrecks, obstructions and other man-made structures. Decision: requires inspection. A potential mine is a high potential hazard; it always requires verification and is never reported as confirmed.

Unknown — Requires Verification

Objects without enough evidence for a reliable class. The system says “unknown” instead of guessing. Decision: requires verification.

Potential risk is expressed as a priority for the operator to review; the system does not determine danger with certainty. The decision engine in this prototype is rule-based and explainable. Trained recognition models are future development and are not part of it.

Verification and background comparison

Multi-pass verification Conceptual

  1. Primary pass (Pass A)
  2. Target detected
  3. Orthogonal pass (Pass B), perpendicular heading
  4. Evidence comparison
  5. Decision update

The grid is surveyed twice: a complete horizontal lawn-mower pass, then a complete perpendicular pass over the same area. Every target is therefore observed from two survey headings about 90° apart (multi-aspect observation), and the two observations are compared before the decision is updated. A second heading adds evidence; it does not guarantee a correct classification. No trained model is involved.

Environmental / acoustic background Conceptual

A detected return is compared with the surrounding seafloor and water column. A return that stands out from its background is stronger evidence that an object is present. In the demo this comparison is a simulated evidence item; no trained anomaly-detection model is claimed.

Data transfer Conceptual

Radio links carry limited data, especially over satellite, so the concept keeps the large raw sonar data on board and sends compact target records first.
